STELLAR SEARCH-PRIORITY SCORE v0.1

Purpose: rank targets for a search for potentially habitable planets, using only
temperature, metallicity, the spectrum, luminosity and radius. This is an explicit
heuristic baseline, NOT a calibrated probability of hosting a planet or life.
No light curves, ages, radial velocities, surface gravities, masses, distances,
activity measurements or planet labels enter the ranking formula.

Formula (T in kelvin; R and L in solar units; Z = [Fe/H] in dex):

  A = exp[-0.5 ((T - 5200)/1500)^2]
  D = max( |log10(R/R_MS(T))|/0.30,
           |log10(L/L_MS(T))|/0.60 )
  B = exp[-0.5 D^2]
  C = 0.9 + 0.1 tanh((Z + 0.5)/0.5)
  Priority = 100 * A^0.4 * B^0.5 * C^0.1

A: a broad preference for late-G/K-like temperatures. The center and width are
   chosen study-design parameters, not measured habitability thresholds. Cooler
   M stars and hotter F stars are NOT asserted to be incapable of hosting life.
B: resemblance to the empirical dwarf/main-sequence radius and luminosity at
   the measured temperature. It downweights inflated/evolved-star-like targets
   for this particular long-lived-dwarf search strategy. It does not prove a
   star's evolutionary state. Giants can have planets and habitable zones.
C: a deliberately weak, bounded metallicity preference. It cannot force a
   metal-poor star's score to zero. Small rocky planets occur over a broad
   metallicity range; the strong giant-planet metallicity law is NOT used here.

R_MS(T), L_MS(T) come from the saved Mamajek dwarf sequence, version 2022.04.16,
interpolated in log temperature/log radius/log luminosity. The modern table
includes updates beyond Pecaut & Mamajek (2013). The reference sequence is not
metallicity-specific; applying it to very metal-poor stars is a limitation.
The 0.30 and 0.60 dex scales are chosen tolerances, not measurement uncertainties.
Taking the maximum avoids treating correlated radius and luminosity offsets as
independent evidence. T, R and L themselves remain strongly dependent.

HOW THE SPECTRUM IS USED
Temperature, [Fe/H] and spectral type come from LAMOST spectral analysis. The full
measured spectrum is preserved. We do not invent an additional independent
"spectrum habitability" signal or double-count spectral type and temperature.
Signal-to-noise, masks and parameter errors are reliability information reported
separately. A poor observation does not make the physical star less habitable.
Scores with flagged inputs remain provisional and require review.

UNCERTAINTY AND ROBUSTNESS
Missing radius/luminosity is missing, never replaced with a value that improves
the score. Only complete inputs receive a numerical score. The output contains
sensitivity bounds from changing temperature preference centers (4800,5200,5600 K),
main-sequence tolerances (0.2,0.3,0.4 dex in radius; twice those in luminosity),
and removing the metallicity preference. These are sensitivity ranges, NOT
confidence intervals. Correlated Gaia posteriors and catalog systematic errors
are not fully propagated. Review large disagreements with L = R^2 (T/5772)^4.

INTERPRETATION
A score of 80 means high priority under these chosen assumptions. It does NOT
mean an 80% chance of a planet, habitability, or life. No planets were detected.
The available features cannot establish stellar activity, binarity, age, planetary
orbits, water or atmospheres. To turn this into a host probability would require
appropriate labeled data, selection-function treatment, calibration, and held-out
validation. An RL model should be evaluated on recovered signals, not rewarded
merely for reproducing this hand-written score.
